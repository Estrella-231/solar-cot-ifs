      Cloud-Optical-Thickness-Assisted Estimation of
    Surface Solar Irradiance from Geostationary Satellite
                        Observations

                                                 Abstract
          Cloud optical thickness (COT) provides an explicit cloud-property representation that can
      complement multispectral imagery when estimating surface irradiance. We investigate its in-
      cremental value for station-level global horizontal irradiance (GHI), with synchronous satellite
      estimation as the primary task and short-term forecasting as an application. A cloud-product-
      supervised retriever generates a COT field from AGRI observations. A compact GHI network
      combines aligned multispectral, solar-geometric, and COT features within a 3 × 3 station neigh-
      bourhood. Matched imagery-only and additional-capacity controls test whether the practical
      input gain exceeds that of a similarly sized image-derived branch. In Hunan validation, the three-
      seed, station-equal RMSE decreased from 123.980 to 120.240 W m−2 (3.02%); in the separately
      trained Hubei validation cohort it decreased from 74.333 to 70.993 W m−2 (4.49%; paired date-
      block 95% interval for COT minus AGRI: [−5.800, −1.118] W m−2 ). These are region-specific
      validation comparisons and are not pooled across different ground-label sampling contracts or
      treated as independent-test evidence. In Hunan’s CPP-supported thick-cloud subgroup (173 ex-
      amples over 25 dates), RMSE decreased from 74.929 to 58.105 W m−2 (22.45%). In a separate
      parameter-matched signed-residual validation, adding COT to an AGRI residual model changed
      RMSE by +2.142 W m−2 (95% interval [−0.688, +4.745]), leaving its incremental value unre-
      solved against this stronger predictor. On the available CPP-referenced local 3 × 3 support, the
      original SimVP-to-retriever forecast had a station-and-lead macro RMSE of 10.664, compared
      with 20.487 for last-frame persistence. Pooled thick-cloud CSI was 0.423 for the original fore-
      cast and 0.306 for persistence. Three equal-budget loss fine-tuning candidates and a single-seed
      direct COT-forecast screen did not improve on the original forecast; the selected direct model’s
      full-patch RMSE was 12.407 versus 10.953 for SimVP-to-retriever. In a matched forecast-input
      validation over 22 target dates and three seeds, the image-only and image-plus-COT heads had
      station-equal RMSEs of 77.150 and 78.155 W m−2 , respectively (difference +1.005, date-block
      95% interval [−0.722, +3.780]); this validation did not show a GHI gain from forecast COT and
      is not an independent test. On common Hubei validation support, the frozen-retriever local-
      feature head and explicit-COT head had station-equal RMSEs of 72.785 and 72.867 W m−2 ,
      respectively (COT minus local features = +0.082, two-day-block 95% interval [−1.490, +1.713]).
      Therefore, the Hubei validation does not isolate a COT-specific increment. All results remain
      validation evidence; no independent test result is reported.


1    Introduction
Satellite irradiance estimation connects cloud observations to the radiation received at a ground
station. A multispectral model can learn this mapping directly, but its inputs describe spectral
responses rather than a single explicit measure of cloud optical thickness. This motivates a practical
question: does adding a retrieved COT representation improve GHI estimation when the original
imagery and solar geometry are already available?
    Multispectral irradiance models use spectral and spatial information to estimate surface radia-
tion. Chen et al. compared selected spectral inputs and attention-based image representations, and
examined their errors under different sky conditions [5]. Physical information can also enter the
mapping through explicit constraints: Sasaki et al. addressed ambiguity associated with surface

reflection and thin clouds using radiative constraints and scene-dependent estimation [6]. These ap-
proaches motivate testing a more specific question here: whether a retrieved COT field adds useful
structure to an irradiance model that already receives the underlying multispectral observations.
    Cloud properties also connect current irradiance estimation to short-term forecasting. Pa-
pachristopoulou et al. evaluated cloud-related estimation errors separately from cloud-motion-based
forecast performance [7]. Recent studies forecast satellite-derived cloud optical depth and use it in
irradiance forecasting: Peng et al. combine optical-depth and cloud-mask forecasts with a radiative-
transfer-based irradiance estimator [8]; a 2026 study forecasts COT from Meteosat imagery and
evaluates its radiative effect through the cloud modification factor [9]. Cloud-FNO evaluates six-
hour forecasts of multiple cloud physical properties, including COT, against an independent test set
[10]. These studies establish active links between cloud-property forecasting and solar applications,
but differ in satellite, target, spatial support, horizon, and irradiance endpoint. The question here is
narrower: whether retrieved COT from the same AGRI observations adds value to matched station-
level GHI estimation, and whether that value is retained when a fixed cloud-image extrapolation
chain supplies future inputs.
    We examine COT as an additional representation learned under cloud-product supervision.
Both the imagery-only and COT-augmented models receive the same local AGRI observations and
deterministic geometry. The latter also receives a co-registered COT field produced by a separately
trained, frozen retriever. COT therefore adds a supervised representation of the available imagery
rather than an independent satellite observation. Its usefulness is assessed by the reduction in GHI
error under matched data and training conditions.
    We address three linked questions. First, does retrieved COT improve matched station-level
GHI estimation beyond multispectral AGRI and solar geometry, beyond a similarly sized imagery-
derived branch, and beyond features from the frozen retriever itself? Second, does this increment
recur in independently trained Hunan and Hubei regional evaluations, and how does it vary across
CPP-referenced optical-thickness strata? Third, when a fixed SimVP chain supplies predicted
imagery, how accurately can its retrieved COT be forecast, and what can the available downstream
diagnostics establish about GHI forecasting? The final question is treated as an application and
an evidence boundary: COT skill and GHI skill are evaluated separately, and input-replacement
diagnostics are not interpreted as a trained forecast-system gain.


2     Study Regions and Data
2.1   Regional design and observations
The Hunan validation cohort contains Sili and Zhujia, and the Hubei validation cohort contains
Niushou, Wangzhai, and Mizhuang. We report the regional validation results separately and do not
interpret them as zero-shot geographic transfer. Regional scores are interpreted within each label
contract and are not pooled as if ground observations had identical sampling.
    Figure 1 locates the five stations and summarizes the first-to-last dates available for AGRI/GHI
samples and CPP-supervised retrieval. The current AGRI/GHI record spans 2 April 2024–30 June
2026 in Hunan and 1 August 2025–14 July 2026 in Hubei. CPP-supervised retrieval support ends
on 2 March 2026 in both regions and is sparse within these date ranges; the endpoints do not imply
continuous daily coverage.

values are taken from the region-specific geolocated grid. Station coordinates identify the relevant
grid cell; the same spatial indices are used for AGRI, geometry, and COT. The COT retriever uses

2.3   Temporal partitions and sample definitions
The Hunan partition reserves separate consecutive two-day validation and test blocks within each
sufficiently covered month, with a one-day buffer on either side of each block. Remaining dates are
used for training; months with fewer than twenty eligible days are training-only. Normalization uses
training dates alone. Retrieval training and GHI evaluation follow the same regional date mapping.
    Synchronous examples are unique (s, τ ) pairs. The completed GHI cohort contains 50,458 train-
ing and 4,479 validation examples, with 2,128 validation examples at Sili and 2,351 at Zhujia.
Forecast examples additionally carry initialization time t: eight observed frames ending at t predict
targets τk = t + k∆, for k = 1, . . . , 16 and ∆ = 15 minutes. Their history and target frames must
all belong to the same partition. Sample support is reported separately for these tasks because a
valid synchronous example need not belong to a complete forecast sequence. All numerical results
currently reported below are validation results.

3.4   Fixed extrapolation and downstream error propagation
The forecasting pathway applies a region-specific, fixed SimVP-family model [3, 4] to eight historical
frames and deterministic geometry for the history and sixteen target times. The Hunan experiments
use the Hunan predictor; Hubei uses its separately trained Hubei predictor and frozen COT retriever:

Here the indices advance in 15-minute steps. The regional input is 256 × 256. The implemented
predictor uses two shared eight-frame prediction steps: the first consumes observed AGRI and
historical geometry, and the second consumes the first eight predictions and their geometry. A
shared 1 × 1 projection from three geometry channels to thirteen AGRI channels adds target-time
conditioning to each predicted segment. Spatial and temporal hidden widths are 32 and 256, with
four spatial stages and eight temporal blocks. The Hunan predictor was trained from random
initialization for twelve epochs using valid-pixel normalized AGRI MSE; checkpoint selection used
season-equal validation pixel RMSE. Hubei forecast-head training used five-fold date-grouped out-
of-fold predicted-AGRI/COT inputs; Hubei validation used the frozen full-training S-to-R prediction
bank.
    Predictive geometry is known from time and location and does not require future cloud obser-
vations. Predicted AGRI is inverse-standardized to physical channel values and then transformed
with the retriever’s own normalization. The same local crop and GHI normalization are applied.
    The matched forecasting comparison trains imagery-only (FA), image-plus-COT (FAC), and
capacity-control (FAP) heads on forecast inputs and evaluates them on identical station–initialization–
target triples. FAP replaces the COT branch with a learned 32 → 4 projection of the forecast-AGRI
feature map; its 24,613 parameters are within 0.51% of FAC. In the current Hubei validation,
station-equal RMSEs were 77.150, 78.155, and 78.980 W m−2 for FA, FAC, and FAP, respec-
tively. The paired target-date cluster-bootstrap differences were FAC–FA = +1.005 (95% interval
[−0.722, +3.780]), FAP–FA = +1.830 ([−0.517, +4.778]), and FAP–FAC = +0.825 ([−2.657, +4.402])
W m−2 ; all intervals crossed zero. Thus, this validation did not resolve a GHI gain from forecast
COT or from the added AGRI-derived branch. A separate diagnostic holds an observed-input
AGRI + COT head fixed and crosses observed versus predicted AGRI with observed-input versus
predicted-input COT. This measures sensitivity to input replacement, but also exposes the head
to a changed input distribution. It therefore quantifies error propagation rather than the gain of a
trained forecasting system.

4.2   Optical-thickness-dependent gains
The thickness analysis uses the station-centre pixel and the pre-existing COT bins [0, 0.1), [0.1, 5),
[5, 30), and [30, 100] for valid CPP references. These are operational near-zero, thin, moderate, and
thick categories, rather than universal cloud-type definitions. CPP near-zero COT is not equated
with an independent clear-sky classification. Each head is scored on precisely the same rows within
a bin, using Eq. (10), and counts are reported by station and target date. No whole-patch mean is
used to assign thickness.
     The reference-based analysis includes only valid centre-CPP matches and is accompanied by
an all-cohort analysis grouped by the frozen retriever’s centre COT. The latter describes model-
retrieved thickness, which can itself be erroneous. Bins are not chosen from observed GHI gains.
Subgroup intervals are descriptive and are not adjusted for multiple comparisons; differences be-
tween subgroup point estimates do not by themselves establish a statistically resolved trend across
thickness.

4.3   Forecasting and physical cases
The forecast application is separated into two questions: how well the extrapolated AGRI fields
retain COT information, and whether a GHI head trained on forecast inputs can use that infor-
mation. A fixed-head replacement diagnostic measures sensitivity to forecast AGRI and forecast-

                                                   8
derived COT, but is not a deployable forecast score. Forecast-COT scores are computed only on
source-keyed CPP support; CPP is a sparse retrieval reference rather than independent physical
truth. The matched forecast-input GHI question remains separate.
    Separately trained forecast-input heads were compared on identical station–initialization–target
triples using predicted AGRI with or without retrieved COT. This is a validation-only application
experiment; the independent test remains unopened. For the synchronous event analysis, four
validation sequences were selected without access to GHI values or model errors: one maximum-
reference-COT-change event and one deterministic-hash event per station. Only four summer cases
satisfy the complete 17-frame CPP-reference requirement in the archived product. The event panels
distinguish CPP reference COT from the frozen retriever’s COT supplied to the GHI head; their GHI
curves use observed AGRI and are same-time diagnostics, not forecasts. Neither future-observation
replacement nor reference-mask intervention is treated as a deployable forecasting input.


5     Results
5.1   Retrieval quality of the COT input
CPP is used as a retrieval reference for COT diagnostics, not as independent physical truth. Forecast
COT is evaluated separately below on the available CPP-referenced support.
    On the source-keyed common support, the Hunan retriever had a pooled-pixel patch RMSE
of 9.074 COT, MAE of 4.646, and bias of +0.279 across 356,169 pixels from 1,398 station–time
patches on 46 BJT dates. Its station-equal RMSE was 9.053. The Hubei shared-patch retriever had
an RMSE of 13.357, MAE of 9.465, and bias of −1.303 across 66,304 pixels from 259 frames on 16
BJT dates. These regional scores use different patch and CPP-supervision contracts and are not
a controlled ranking of regional model quality. In both regions, COT was underestimated in the
CPP-reference ≥ 30 stratum: the mean bias was −8.72 (95% date-block interval [−11.53, −5.59];
50,924 pixels on 34 dates) in Hunan and −14.11 ([−19.49, −9.02]; 15,869 pixels on 12 dates) in
Hubei. These thickness-conditioned results are descriptive, with sparse date support in Hubei.

5.2   Overall irradiance estimation performance
On 4,479 common validation examples from Sili and Zhujia, the AGRI baseline and AGRI + COT
obtained three-seed, station-equal RMSEs of 123.980 and 120.240 W m−2 . Adding COT reduced
RMSE by 3.740 W m−2 , or 3.02%, with a paired 95% date-block interval of [−6.180, −1.597] W m−2
for the difference. Both stations improved under each of the three random seeds. Thus, the added
representation improves same-time GHI estimation in the completed Hunan validation comparison.
    In the separately trained Hubei validation cohort, three-seed, station-equal RMSE was 74.333
W m−2 for AGRI, 70.993 W m−2 for AGRI + COT, and 73.839 W m−2 for the additional-capacity
control. The AGRI + COT minus AGRI point difference was −3.340 W m−2 (10,000 paired
date-block resamples; 95% interval [−5.800, −1.118]); the difference from the capacity control was
−2.846 W m−2 (interval [−5.147, −0.717]). AGRI + COT had lower validation RMSE than AGRI
and the capacity control in eight of nine station-by-seed point comparisons. A consecutive two-
day-block sensitivity gave intervals of [−6.139, −0.729] and [−5.504, −0.349] W m−2 for these two
comparisons, respectively. We also compared an additional arm that replaces the explicit COT input
with a similarly sized local feature branch from the frozen retriever’s 16-channel decoder feature
map (AL). On the 486 target times shared across all three stations (1,458 station-target rows) over
21 dates, RMSE was 72.867 W m−2 for AGRI + COT and 72.785 W m−2 for AL (COT minus
AL = +0.082 W m−2 ; 2,000 consecutive two-day-block resamples; 95% interval [−1.490, +1.713]).
The Hubei AGRI + COT and AL heads had 24,489 and 24,549 trainable parameters, respectively.
This interval spans zero, so the validation evidence does not distinguish explicit COT from the
retriever-feature control. All intervals are conditional on validation-based checkpoint selection and
are not independent test confirmation.
    Figure 4 shows three-seed station means and paired date-block intervals for both regions. Values
are not pooled across regions because ground-label time sampling differs.
    The Hunan increment was also present at station-centre solar altitudes of 45◦ or higher: three-
seed, station-equal RMSE was 154.111 W m−2 for AGRI and 148.645 W m−2 for AGRI + COT
(1,527 station–target examples over 35 dates; paired difference −5.465 W m−2 , 95% date-block
interval [−8.990, −2.152]). On the 30 dates with complete daylight coverage at both Hunan stations,
station- and seed-equal daily irradiation MAE decreased from 0.5491 to 0.5032 kWh m−2 (difference
−0.0459, 95% interval [−0.0861, −0.0067]). The daily bias shifted from −0.0164 to −0.0561 kWh
m−2 , indicating a more negative signed error despite the lower absolute error. The capacity control
remained close to AGRI alone (153.854 W m−2 high-sun RMSE; 0.5479 kWh m−2 daily irradiation
MAE). These Hunan validation diagnostics do not imply a photovoltaic generation gain and are
not pooled with Hubei.

5.3   Gain by reference optical thickness
Valid station-centre CPP matches covered 1,284 of the 4,479 GHI validation examples and 46 target
dates. Figure 5 shows the Hunan thickness-stratified comparison; Table S1 gives the paired values.
The bins use the station-centre CPP COT and the same rows for both GHI models. The thick-
reference group contained 173 examples across 25 dates. Its RMSE decreased from 74.929 to 58.105
W m−2 , a reduction of 16.824 W m−2 or 22.45%; all three seeds improved. The lower-thickness
groups had smaller point-estimate reductions, approximately 1.5–1.7%, with intervals spanning zero.
    The thick-reference improvement varied by station: Sili RMSE decreased from 69.870 to 64.661
W m−2 , while Zhujia decreased from 79.987 to 51.549 W m−2 . Positive GHI bias was reduced
from 14.460 to 1.648 W m−2 at Sili and from 48.356 to 19.432 W m−2 at Zhujia. These results are
consistent with a reduction of irradiance overestimation within this reference-thick subgroup. They
do not imply a uniform correction for all clouds or establish a causal thickness-response law.

5.4      Cloud-event analysis
The error-blind event audit links observed AGRI false-colour composites, CPP reference COT,
frozen-R COT, and interval-mean GHI for four 16-lead summer validation sequences. Across these
selected cases, the three-seed mean of the within-sequence RMSE was 213.851 versus 218.408 W m−2
(AGRI baseline versus AGRI + COT) for the Sili maximum-change case, 151.268 versus 150.397 for
the Sili hash-selected case, 129.933 versus 138.871 for the Zhujia maximum-change case, and 126.700
versus 136.617 for the Zhujia hash-selected case. Thus, COT slightly reduced error in one selected
sequence and increased it in three; these descriptive case scores have no event-level uncertainty
interval and are not independent samples.
    In the Sili maximum-change case (9 July 2025, 13:15 BJT), CPP and R station-pixel COT
both rose and later declined, while the GHI heads changed more smoothly than the observed 15-
minute means during a rapid irradiance drop. This juxtaposition is consistent with unresolved
timing or amplitude errors in the station mapping, but a single event cannot identify their cause.
The selected cases therefore illustrate event-level heterogeneity rather than explain the aggregate
COT gain. They cover summer only because the archived CPP product lacks complete 15-minute
reference sequences in the other seasons.

5.5      Role of the COT representation
The additional-capacity control obtained an RMSE of 123.822 W m−2 , close to the AGRI baseline.
AGRI + COT improved on this control by 3.582 W m−2 (95% interval [−5.977, −1.479]). The
gain therefore exceeds that obtained by adding a comparably sized imagery-derived branch in this
experiment.
    A separate, parameter-matched signed-residual experiment provides a stronger AGRI compar-
ison. The AGRI residual model achieved 111.277 W m−2 , while its AGRI + COT counterpart
achieved 113.419 W m−2 . The COT-minus-AGRI difference was +2.142 W m−2 (95% paired date-
block interval [−0.688, +4.745]), which does not resolve an incremental gain or loss. Both arms
improved over the frozen baseline, but the added COT did not improve the point estimate. This
is a validation-only same-time retrieval comparison; training uses fold-specific out-of-fold baseline
predictions whereas validation uses the full-data baseline prediction, a documented distribution shift
that limits interpretation.

5.6   Application to short-term forecasting
On the available CPP-referenced local 3 × 3 support, original SimVP forecast COT achieved a
station-and-lead-macro RMSE of 10.664. Last-frame persistence had RMSE 20.487. Pooled thick-
cloud CSI was 0.423 for the original forecast and 0.306 for persistence. CPP is a sparse retrieval
reference, not independent physical truth. Three equal-budget fine-tuning losses did not improve
the original model: station-and-lead-macro RMSE was 10.750 for MSE, 10.756 for temporal-change
loss, and 10.759 for spatial-gradient loss. Their candidate-minus-original RMSE differences were
+0.086, +0.092, and +0.095, respectively. Thus this screen supports the original forecast over
persistence on the available CPP support, but provides no evidence that these loss changes improve
it. Figure 7 shows the station-wise COT lead curves and does not display GHI forecast skill; the
separate matched forecast-input GHI validation is summarized below. A separate single-seed screen
asked whether forecasting retrieved COT directly from its eight-frame history could improve on
the fixed SimVP-forecast-AGRI-to-retriever chain. Both direct models predicted 16 future COT
maps over the full 16 × 16 patch. One used future AGRI-derived retriever COT as its training
target; the other substituted CPP values where available and used retriever COT elsewhere. They
were evaluated on the same 3,140 validation sequences and CPP-common support (46 target dates;
5,224,460 patch pixels). The validation-selected CPP/R hybrid scored 12.407 COT RMSE, versus
12.512 for the R-target model and 10.953 for the fixed SimVP-to-retriever baseline. On the station-
centred local 3×3 support, the corresponding values were 12.162, 12.275, and 10.664. For the hybrid
model, station–lead-macro RMSE stratified by CPP reference thickness was 8.988 for COT below
1, 5.140 for 1–5, 9.594 for 5–30, and 25.668 for COT at least 30 (2,119,268, 575,625, 1,831,804,
and 697,763 patch pixels, respectively). The direct models therefore did not improve the point
scores, with the largest remaining error in the thick-reference stratum. This is a one-seed validation
screen with model selection on the same validation cohort; the point differences are descriptive, not
inferential. CPP remains a retrieval reference rather than independent physical truth. The screen
evaluates COT only and does not test a GHI head trained on forecast inputs.
    To test whether a larger gradient on absolute COT errors could address the thick-reference
deficit, we added a fixed physical-amplitude MSE term to the hybrid model’s log-space objective,
leaving the training data, architecture, seed, and budget unchanged. On the same validation sup-
port, the selected model scored 12.473 RMSE on the full patch and 12.209 on the local 3 × 3,
slightly worse than the base hybrid (12.407 and 12.162) and still above the fixed SimVP-to-retriever
reference (10.953 and 10.664). The COT ≥ 30 stratum improved by only 0.133 and 0.358 RMSE,
respectively, relative to the hybrid base, while the overall scores did not improve. This one-seed
screen therefore does not support absolute-amplitude weighting as a remedy; it does not distinguish
thickness errors from cloud-position or timing errors, and it provides no GHI forecast evidence.
    We next decomposed every unique validation episode in which station-pixel CPP COT was at
least 30 for two or more consecutive valid 15-minute targets. The seven episodes (four at Sili and
three at Zhujia) occurred on only three BJT dates; for each episode we scored the longest-lead
validation forecast that covered the complete event. On the local 3 × 3 support, mean event RMSE
for SimVP-to-retriever was 19.53 at Sili and 13.48 at Zhujia, compared with 6.87 and 7.38 when the
same retriever was applied to observed future AGRI as a hindsight diagnostic. Pooled thick-cloud
CSI was 0.149 and 0.000 for SimVP-to-retriever, versus 0.814 and 0.617 for the hindsight diagnostic.
The direct CPP/R-hybrid COT forecaster had mean event RMSE 24.47 and 26.79, respectively. The
event signatures vary: SimVP-to-retriever detected some Sili episodes but missed the largest one
and all three Zhujia episodes. The observed-future-AGRI diagnostic also retains nonzero error and
is not deployable. These seven episodes, concentrated on three dates, are descriptive rather than
independent statistical evidence; CPP remains a retrieval reference, so they motivate but do not

prove a forecast-image bottleneck or its cause.
    To separate forecasted-image evolution from retrieval response, we compared adjacent 15-min
COT changes from SimVP-forecast AGRI and observed future AGRI, both processed by the same
frozen retriever. Across 52 BJT target dates (423,900 local pixel transitions per station), forecast-
to-retrieval changes were correlated with the observed-AGRI retrieval response (Pearson r = 0.533
at Sili and 0.593 at Zhujia), but the through-origin slopes were 0.334 and 0.392, indicating atten-
uated change amplitude relative to this proxy. Relative to last-frame persistence, model-minus-
persistence change-RMSE differences were −1.737 (95% date-block interval [−2.396, −1.212]) and
−2.184 ([−3.076, −1.494]) at the two stations. Relative to the original SimVP trajectory, temporal-
change fine-tuning produced a change-RMSE difference of −0.046 at Sili (95% interval [−0.085, −0.013])
and −0.013 at Zhujia ([−0.047, +0.018]). This is a retrieval-response diagnostic, not an evaluation
against physical COT truth, because both trajectories are produced by the same retriever. On the
much smaller support where CPP was valid at both adjacent leads (seven BJT dates per station),
forecast-to-retrieval changes had weak association with CPP changes (r = 0.133 at Sili and 0.019
at Zhujia), whereas the observed-AGRI retrieval response had higher correlations (r = 0.650 and
0.608). This sparse, retrieval-based CPP support cannot identify whether the discrepancy is caused
by forecasted cloud evolution, retrieval error, or reference-product variability; these proxy results
are mechanistic evidence only.
    In a separate fixed-head sensitivity diagnostic over 2,817 validation sequences and 52 target
dates, station-equal GHI RMSE was 118.14 W m−2 for observed future AGRI with contemporane-
ous retrieved COT, 125.42 when only COT was replaced by COT retrieved from SimVP-forecast
AGRI, 126.46 for forecast AGRI with contemporaneous COT, and 139.39 when both inputs were
forecast-derived. Replacing COT increased RMSE by 7.28 W m−2 (95% paired date-block interval
[4.52, 10.54]) with observed future AGRI and by 12.93 W m−2 ([9.73, 16.58]) with forecast AGRI.
The former inputs include future observations and are an offline reference; all four scores come
from a head trained on observed inputs. This diagnoses error propagation and input-distribution
sensitivity, not an intrinsic failure of COT.
    In the matched forecast-input validation, separately trained image-only (FA) and image-plus-
COT (FAC) heads were evaluated on the same 22 target dates, station–initialization–target keys,
and observed GHI, using three seeds and three stations. The station-equal RMSE was 77.150 W m−2
for FA and 78.155 W m−2 for FAC (FAC–FA = +1.005 W m−2 , a 1.30% increase); one of the three
seed-level station-equal comparisons favoured FAC. The paired one-day target-date and consecutive
two-day-block bootstraps each used 10,000 resamples and gave 95% intervals of [−0.722, +3.780]
and [−0.681, +3.813] W m−2 , respectively. This is a documented precision revision from the 2,000-
resample default in the general protocol; the resampling unit and seed are recorded with the result
artifact. Thus, this validation does not show that adding forecast COT improves GHI prediction.
The intervals are conditional on validation-based model selection; no independent test result is
reported.

6    Discussion
The evidence supports a conditional claim about synchronous estimation. In the compact matched
heads, adding local retrieved COT reduced validation RMSE relative to AGRI alone in both inde-
pendently trained regions. The Hunan capacity control did not reproduce the full Hunan gain. In
Hubei, however, the frozen-retriever feature control matched the explicit-COT head on common sup-
port, so the validation result does not isolate a COT-specific increment there. The CPP-supported
thick-reference subgroup showed the largest Hunan point improvement, but it is small and sparse;
CPP is a retrieval reference rather than independent physical truth. Moreover, the stronger signed-
residual comparison did not resolve an incremental COT benefit. The evidence therefore supports a
useful supervised representation in the tested compact Hunan head, while the Hubei control leaves
open whether explicit COT adds value beyond features already present in the retriever.
    The forecasting evidence is weaker and addresses a different endpoint. The original SimVP-to-
retriever chain outperformed last-frame COT persistence on the available local CPP support, but
temporal-change and spatial-gradient fine-tuning did not improve CPP-referenced RMSE. Direct
COT forecasting and its fixed absolute-amplitude loss screen also failed to improve the overall point
score over the fixed SimVP-to-retriever baseline. Across seven station-pixel thick-cloud episodes
concentrated on three dates, the chain missed all three Zhujia episodes and the largest Sili episode;
applying the same retriever to observed future AGRI performed better, but this hindsight diagnostic
is not deployable and still disagrees with CPP. The episode evidence therefore motivates improving
predicted cloud imagery, while remaining too limited to identify a unique cause or establish a
population-level effect.
    The matched forecast-input validation closes the earlier evaluation gap but provides no resolved
GHI gain from either forecast COT or the added AGRI-derived branch: FAC and FAP had higher
point RMSEs than FA, while all paired date-block intervals crossed zero. The intervals do not
establish equivalence or degradation. This is a validation result conditional on selection, not a
final independent test. The Hunan high-solar-altitude and complete-day diagnostics show lower
absolute errors alongside a more negative daily bias. These secondary results reinforce the need to
retain region- and condition-specific evaluation. Independent testing is still required before broader
generalization or deployment claims.




                                                 18
7    Conclusion
The Hubei AGRI-to-AGRI+COT improvement is not by itself evidence that explicit COT adds
information beyond the frozen retriever: on common validation support, the matched retriever-
feature control performed similarly, and its paired interval included zero. This limits the COT-
specific interpretation of the Hubei result. Retrieved COT lowered validation GHI estimation error
in the separately trained Hunan and Hubei cohorts; the Hunan capacity control suggests that the
Hunan gain was not explained solely by adding a similarly sized image-derived branch. These
regional results remain validation evidence, and the Hubei evidence is limited to three stations
rather than a broad regional climate sample. The largest Hunan point improvement occurred in the
sparse CPP-supported thick-reference subgroup, while a stronger signed-residual comparison did not
resolve an incremental benefit. For forecasting, the fixed SimVP-to-retriever chain beat last-frame
persistence on the available CPP support, but the tested loss changes and direct COT forecasts did
not improve its overall COT RMSE. In the matched forecast-input GHI validation, FA, FAC, and
FAP scored 77.150, 78.155, and 78.980 W m−2 , respectively. Neither FAC nor FAP showed a resolved
improvement over FA: the date-block 95% intervals for FAC–FA ([−0.722, +3.780]) and FAP–FA
([−0.517, +4.778] W m−2 ) crossed zero. Event analysis identifies missed thick-cloud episodes and
motivates improving the upstream cloud-image forecast, without establishing a unique cause. No
independent test result is available.
